Designed around control

Credibility comes from showing what was checked—and what was not.

MCAS is designed to help you make better-informed decisions, not to manufacture certainty or send unsolicited messages behind your back.

Evidence before outreach

  • Source links are retained with prospect research.
  • Website status is based on the response observed during a check.
  • Restricted or unavailable checks are not labelled as broken.
  • AI content is a draft, not proof of a claim.

You stay in charge

  • Each customer signs in with their own Google account and workspace.
  • Gmail integration is optional and user-connected.
  • MCAS does not read incoming email or invoke a Gmail send operation.
  • You decide what to edit, approve, copy or send yourself.
Website checks are limited public-page assessments, not penetration tests, legal compliance reviews or security certifications. Gmail's gmail.compose permission is broader than draft creation, even though MCAS uses it only to create drafts. Google verification is required before a general public Gmail integration launch.
Frequently asked questions

Know what MCAS does—and where it draws the line.

Who is MCAS designed for?

Independent web developers, freelancers and small digital agencies that want to research potential clients, understand a website opportunity and prepare a relevant proposal or first conversation.

Is MCAS a freelance marketplace like Upwork?

No. MCAS is a private client-acquisition workspace for your own business. It does not list freelancers for hire, broker contracts or process client payments.

Does MCAS guarantee that a business needs a website?

No. Public listings can be incomplete. A missing website URL is labelled as “no website link found” until you independently verify the business's online presence.

Does MCAS send emails or read my inbox?

No. It can create Gmail drafts after you connect your account. It does not read incoming messages or include a send endpoint. You open Gmail and send messages yourself.

Are website audits security tests?

No. They inspect a bounded set of public pages and selected technical signals. They do not exploit vulnerabilities, bypass logins, scan ports or certify a website as secure.

Can I start without buying a domain?

Yes, you can test the local development version. A public customer launch requires a deployed HTTPS address, appropriate policies and completed Google verification steps.
